---

I’ve loved taking things apart and tinkering ever since I was a kid. I was always drawn to electronics in general. My career technically started when I was four, when I managed to ruin the family’s beloved cassette player. There’s even a photo of it, but I’m way too shy to show that. Let’s just say my family wasn’t exactly thrilled with my *repair* work.

When I was five, I really took a liking to my brother’s water-filled watch. And once I got my hands on it, obviously I had to figure out how to get the water out. My brilliant idea was to smash it full force against the wall. Let’s just say they weren’t thrilled about that one either.

A lot of years have passed since then, and I can proudly say my repair skills have improved a lot, though I still make ~~mistakes~~ **happy accidents** here and there. In today's video, I’m trying to bring a few half-forgotten Sega quartz games back to life. But before we go any further:

>**YES**, I know these are technically LCD games, but here in Hungary we call them “quartz games.” It’s a nostalgia thing really, so I’ll keep using that term even though it’s not exactly correct or common anywhere else. (Also… I only realized this after finishing the video, but let’s just pretend my earlier explanation was the more elegant reason, okay?)
>

<p>
I first came across these games back in primary school. The one I used to play with between classes was <i>AiAi Banana Catch</i>. That’s the LCD game you can see on the video’s thumbnail too. In the first promotional run there were <b>six</b> games released in total, and I’ve managed to get my hands on three of them. The other three are pretty elusive. I haven’t seen any of them pop up for sale in Hungary yet, but hey, patience is a virtue, I guess. For taking them apart, restoring them and putting them back together, I found a great site called <a href="https://pcburn.com/mcsega-teardown-of-mcdonalds-sega-handheld-game/">PCBurn</a>. (I still absolutely love that pun!) Unfortunately, one of them has its wires snapped, so it doesn’t make any sound. But I promise I’ll fix it. I haven’t done any soldering in quite a while, so I hope the skill’s still in my hands… but as the saying goes: if it doesn’t work, just use more flux.
</p>

<p>
You can also click the image to visit the page about the promotions. It has all the released games listed, along with the key info for each of them, plus tons of flyers and other promotional material. Did you know there was even a Pakistani TV <a href="https://info.sonicretro.org/images/f/f3/Pakistan_LCD_McDonald%27s_commercial_2004.mp4">commercial</a>? The 3D animation they made for the games is straight-up nightmare fuel. They <b>really</b> did Knuckles dirty. Another fun fact is that one of the games had a special silver edition and they only made 2,500 of them. It was handed out randomly with Happy Meals. It was so rare that for a while people weren’t even sure it actually existed. There’s proof now that it does, but so far I’ve only ever seen <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j2gBEg3S1Ds">one</a> working unit.
</p>
